The loop

Four seconds from windshield to Registry.

No forms, no typing, no guessing what you're looking at. The whole game is one tap and the thing that happens after it.

STEP 01

Spot it

Something interesting rolls past or sits under a tarp. Open the app — it lands on the camera, because that's the only screen that matters in the moment.

STEP 02

Shoot it

One tap. The photo goes to a vision model that identifies make, model, and era, judges rarity, and grades your framing with a note on how to shoot it better.

STEP 03

File it

The verdict lands with a chime and the car enters your Registry — numbered, dated, and yours. Miss the ID? Correct it in the dossier. The record is still yours.

The Registry

Collectors have kept registries for a century.

Marque registries track every known example of a car, chassis by chassis. This is the street version — a numbered record of the metal you found yourself, on the day you found it. Cars and bikes keep separate books.

Why it counts

A collection is only worth something if it's honest.

Anyone can screenshot a supercar. These are the rules that make an entry mean you were standing there.

You have to be there

The shutter opens your camera — gallery imports don't count. The verifier flags photos of screens, magazines and press shots. Flagged captures still save to your garage, they just don't score.

Duplicates are detected

Every photo gets a fingerprint on the server, checked against every other hunter's. The same car shot twice is one entry.

The judge runs server-side

Identification happens on our servers, not on your phone where it could be edited. Rate limits live there too.

What you can do with it

Built for the way people actually chase metal.

Documentation

Full dossiers

One photo names it. Five angles — interior, badge, rear, engine, detail — make it a dossier. Each is graded, and badge shots get the script read back to you.

Owners

Bounties

Own something people want to see? Add it to your Stable and post a bounty. Hunters who spot it in the wild get credit, and you see who found you.

Barn finds

Sleeping cars

Flat tyres, moss, a decade of dust. Dormant metal gets its own classification and a title to chase.

Lost to history

Most Wanted

Machines the record says were built but nobody can find — Bugatti's missing Atlantic, a Ferrari 375 MM, Dean's Spyder. Spot something that fits and you file a lead.
